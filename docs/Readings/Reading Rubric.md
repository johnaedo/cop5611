When reading the papers, keep the follow questions in mind:

-   What is the problem and why does it matter?
-   What is the solution and how is it new/different?
-   What are the contributions and limitations?

For the summary of each paper, write one concise review paragraph.  

## Submission Format
- Word doc (.docx) or PDF upload.
- 10-11 point type of a standard font (Calibri, Arial, Times New Roman, etc.)
	- If you submit it in Comics Sans, I will send you straight to the Dean's Office!
- Single-space
- 1 page minimum, 2 page max

## Rubric

-   **1 point** - Accurately summarizes the main point of the paper.
-   **3 points** - Discuss at least one technical strength and one weaknesses
		*3 points*:  Demonstrate clear understanding of the work and related the strengths and weaknesses to the work's significance.  Also contextualizes them to relevant current trends and issues where appropriate.
		*2 points*:  Demonstrate basic understanding of the work, but only weakly contextualizes them.
		*1 point*: Strengths and weaknesses are enumerated, but aren't contextualized at all.
	*1 point*: - Provide some form of personal insight, by relating it to your work, research, hobbies, or interests.  This one can be tricky, but be creative (see below)
	

## Hints   
- You can take advantage of hindsight.  Is the technique being used today?  Did it evolve?  If so, how?  Was it abandoned?  If so, why?  What supplanted or obsoleted it?
- Personal insight:
	- Did you draw any insights that could be useful to you in your semester project, dissertation, work, or hobby?
	- Did it inspire you to further research the topic?
	- Did you have a personal experience with the technique or system discussed (or one that was similar)?  Did the paper change your perception of that system?  Maybe you worked with a system that could have benefited from that solution?  If so, how would you have benefited?
